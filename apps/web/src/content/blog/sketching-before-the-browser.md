My first drawing of a page fits in the corner of a notebook. At that size, there is room for a title, a few lines, and a rectangle where an image might go. There is very little room to fuss with a border radius.

I write the actual heading into the sketch. Even in my rough handwriting, a seven-word title takes up more space than a tidy placeholder. It changes where the next section should start.

## Try the narrow version

Next to the first sketch, I draw a narrower one. Two columns become one. A caption that sat beside a picture needs somewhere else to go. If I cannot decide which item should come first, I make that decision while moving it still costs a few pencil strokes.

## Carry one question into the browser

The sketch usually leaves something unresolved. Perhaps the headline competes with the first card, or a long paragraph needs a break. I write that question beside the page and use real content to answer it in the browser.

By the time I open the editor, I have a starting layout and a specific thing to test.
