I start a type scale with a paragraph I actually want someone to read. On a blog, that usually means a few sentences from the middle of a post. An introduction tends to be shorter and more polished than the copy the layout has to carry.

I set the reading width and body size together. If my eye keeps losing the start of the next line, I try a narrower column before changing the font.

## Let the longest heading set a limit

A short title can make almost any display size look good. I put the longest title beside it and open the page at phone width. The moment a single word ends up stranded, I have a concrete decision to make about measure, wrapping, or size.

For this site I let the display size grow with the viewport and stop at a fixed maximum. That keeps the space between the heading and its introduction predictable on a wide screen.

## Name the roles after using them

Once there is a working page, I collect the sizes I used. A post date and a caption can usually share a style. A card title sometimes needs less emphasis than a section heading, even when their sizes are close.

The result is a short list I can remember. When I add a page, I can spend my time deciding which words deserve attention.
